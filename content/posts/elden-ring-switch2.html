Elden Ring on Switch 2 Is the Biggest Gaming Moment of August

After a year-long delay and a lot of scepticism, Tarnished Edition drops August 28. Here's why this one actually matters — and what to expect.

EX
ExoraWorld Team
Gaming & Tech
Aug 12, 2026 4 min read
Elden Ring Switch 2 Gaming Nintendo FromSoftware

Let's be honest — when Elden Ring was delayed from its original Switch 2 launch window, a lot of people wrote it off. The assumption was performance compromises, a messy port, maybe a permanent wait. That assumption looks increasingly wrong.

Elden Ring: Tarnished Edition drops August 28, and it's not just a straight port. It packages the base game with the Shadow of the Erdtree DLC, performance optimisations specifically built for Switch 2's hardware, and a set of handheld-specific UI tweaks that FromSoftware says were developed alongside the port rather than bolted on afterwards. The delay, it seems, was used.

📅 Release Date Elden Ring: Tarnished Edition — August 28, 2026 on Nintendo Switch 2. Includes the full base game + Shadow of the Erdtree DLC.

Why This Is a Bigger Deal Than It Sounds

Switch 2's install base is already enormous. Nintendo's latest has done what the original Switch did — pull in people who don't own a dedicated gaming PC or a current-gen console. For a huge chunk of that audience, Elden Ring was simply not accessible before. Now it is, and portable.

Think about what that means in practice. Elden Ring on the bus. Elden Ring on your lunch break. Elden Ring in bed at 11pm when you have work tomorrow and absolutely shouldn't. The game was already one of the defining experiences of the last console generation. Putting it in a handheld that goes everywhere changes the relationship people have with it.

There's also a genuine case that Switch 2 is a surprisingly capable machine for this kind of port. The hardware is well beyond what the original Switch could do, and Bandai Namco and FromSoftware have had an extra year to work with it. Early reports suggest the performance target is a solid 30fps in handheld mode and a more ambitious 60fps when docked — numbers that, if they hold up, would make this a respectable version of the game rather than a compromise one.

What We're Watching For

The delay bought time. Whether it bought enough time is what August 28 will tell us. The things we're most interested in:

  • Performance consistency — 30fps in handheld mode sounds good on paper. Whether the notoriously demanding open world holds that in the busiest areas is a different question.
  • Load times — Switch 2's SSD is a significant upgrade over the original. Elden Ring's loading screens were never its worst feature, but shorter is always better for a game you die in constantly.
  • The DLC on portable hardware — Shadow of the Erdtree is visually demanding. Seeing it in handheld mode will be one of the more interesting technical things to watch this month.
  • Touch controls — Probably not useful for this game, but FromSoftware said they explored it. Curious to see what, if anything, made the final cut.

August is stacked. Star Wars Zero Company, the Oblivion remaster on Switch 2, Lies of P Complete Edition — there's a lot competing for attention and time this month. But Elden Ring: Tarnished Edition is the one with the longest shadow. It's the game that will define how people talk about Switch 2 ports for the next year.

We'll have a full impressions piece up on launch day. For now — if you haven't played Elden Ring before, August 28 is an excellent time to start. And if you have, Shadow of the Erdtree on a handheld sounds like a very good reason to go back.
